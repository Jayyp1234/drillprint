**Independent refutation attempt (CONFIRMED):** Independently verified the kinematics numerically: rolling-without-slip contact-velocity residual is exactly 0 for center whirl Ω = −ω·d_b/(d_h−d_b), while the spec's Ω = −ω·d_h/(d_h−d_b) leaves a 0.314 m/s residual at the test geometry (not rolling) and instead equals Ω_center − ω, the rate the contact/bending line sweeps in the body frame — the correct observable for a pipe-mounted ACC_LAT sensor. ## Signal processing & matching mathematics (Part II; §18/§22 DSP arithmetic)

### Dimension summary

Part II specifies a Shazam-style pipeline: three STFT profiles (LOW 10 Hz/N=256/hop 64, MID 100/512/128, HIGH 400/1024/256, Hann, 75% overlap) → log-magnitude peak picking (maximum_filter local-max + per-frame median+β gate + 5-peaks/frame cap) → anchor-target pair hashing (f1:10 | Δf:8 signed | Δt:6 = 24 bits, Δt∈[1,32], |Δk|≤63, fan-out 8) → per-episode offset histogram scored by max bin with Poisson significance (λ = matched_pairs/n_bins, alert at confidence 1−p > 0.99 for K=2 evaluations), with order-domain resampling at 64 samples/rev for RPM-clocked classes. The table-level arithmetic is solid — I recomputed and confirmed every Δf (0.0390625/0.1953125/0.390625 Hz), frame span (25.6/5.12/2.56 s), hop=N/4=75% overlap inside the stated 50–75% range, exact COLA for periodic Hann (overlap-add sums 2.000/1.000 at N/4, N/2), sidelobes (rect −13.26 dB, Hann −31.47 dB), the bit budget (10+8+6=24; 513 HIGH rfft bins need exactly 10 bits since 2^9=512; Δt and Δk fit with ~2 bits total slack), order-domain Nyquist 32 covering the order 2–12 whirl band (400 Hz time-fs binds only >375 RPM), the §3 whirl example (2.5×6=15 Hz, 17.5 absolute), the showcase significance example (P(Poisson(2)≥40) underflows double precision), the median gate's rejection of dropout plateaus, and §18's matcher (≤10 ms at 10⁶ hashes; core work ~0.1 ms) and ingest (5×400 Hz trivial for NumPy) targets. But the window/latency/statistics layer above that arithmetic is broken in three load-bearing places: (1) the specified 30 s LOW query window contains exactly ONE complete STFT frame (floor((300−256)/64)+1=1), so stick-slip queries generate zero hashes — the flagship class cannot match at all as written (needs W≈83–230 s or a smaller frame); (2) the ≤5 s stick-slip latency target and gate 1 are physically impossible — the phenomenon's own period is 2–10 s, LOW frames span 25.6 s advancing every 6.4 s, and K=2 debounce adds 2–5 s, giving a realistic floor of ~30–60 s (the ≤3 s whirl target also loses to the debounce's 4–10 s minimum, and the 2-min demo's 35 s stick-slip segment may not even fire); (3) the Poisson decision rule is statistically incoherent without multiple-comparison correction — at the spec's own scale (10⁶ hashes, ~19.6 effective hash bits, not the claimed ~24) λ≈0.06/bin, so 2 coincident random hashes reach confidence 0.998 and ~17 episode-bins cross the 0.99 threshold per evaluation, with overlapping windows defeating the K=2 debounce — gate 2 (zero false alerts/hour) fails as specified. Secondary but real: order-domain resampling omits the mandatory anti-alias low-pass (a legit 150 Hz ring at 120 RPM aliases to order 11, inside the hashable whirl band, RPM-dependently); the whirl synth's 100–300/150–300 Hz rings exceed the 200 Hz Nyquist of the 400 Hz generator; band tables disagree across §2/§4/§5 and partly exceed channel Nyquist; and the MID 10 s window's 4 frames make ~90% of library Δt values unmatchable. Overall: the DSP building blocks and performance targets are correct and comfortably feasible, but the query-window sizing, latency gates, and significance thresholding need spec-level redesign before implementation — each fix is cheap and none threatens the core architecture.

### Findings (all confirmed by adversarial refutation)

#### [CRITICAL] §18 — Latency gates are arithmetically unachievable with §6 hops and §10 evaluation defaults

**Evidence:** §10 alerts require confidence > threshold for K=2 consecutive evaluations, 'evaluated every 2–5 s'. §6 LOW profile produces a new STFT column only every hop = 64/10 = 6.4 s, so consecutive evaluations closer than 6.4 s see identical LOW data; minimum stick-slip alert latency = 2 × max(6.4, eval) = 12.8 s — and since a LOW frame spans 25.6 s, onset energy underfills the first frames, so realistic latency is 15–25 s vs the §18/§22.1 gate of ≤5 s. Whirl/bounce: best case 2 × 2 s eval = 4.0 s vs the ≤3 s gate (10 s at the 5 s cadence). Every row of the computed matrix is VIOLATED: stick-slip 12.8 s > 5 s; whirl 4.0–10.0 s > 3 s; bounce 4.0–10.0 s > 3 s. Acceptance gates §22.1 and the M4 end-to-end test cannot pass as specified.

**Recommendation:** Pick per-profile evaluation cadences tied to hop (e.g., HIGH/MID eval every 1 s with K=2 → ~2–3 s attainable), shrink the LOW hop (e.g., hop=16 → 1.6 s columns) or allow K=1 at a stricter threshold for first alert, and restate the stick-slip gate to something physically honest (≤15–20 s, or latency measured from the first full frame containing onset) — resolving a 0.1–0.5 Hz comb at Δf=0.039 Hz intrinsically requires ~25.6 s frames, so a 5 s gate contradicts the profile design itself.

**Independent refutation attempt (CONFIRMED):** Recomputed independently: LOW hop = 64/10 = 6.4 s, frame span = 256/10 = 25.6 s, Δf = 0.0390625 Hz — all match §6's table. §10 defaults: eval every 2–5 s, confidence > 0.99 for K = 2 consecutive evaluations. Whirl/bounce: pure arithmetic — even at the fastest 2 s cadence, K = 2 makes the alert fire no earlier than the second qualifying evaluation, ≥ ~4 s after onset (2×2.0 = 4.0 s recomputed), and 10 s at the 5 s cadence, vs the §18/§22 gate of ≤ 3 s. No reading of the spec rescues this. Stick-slip: f₀ = 3100/(4·3000) = 0.258 Hz (period ~3.9 s); 5 s of post-onset data spans ~1.3 periods, and a Hann frame of 25.6 s contains at most ~20% dysfunction energy at t=5 s — the 0.039 Hz-resolution odd-harmonic comb the library hashes encode cannot exist in the query frames yet, so 0.99 confidence + K=2 within 5 s (§22 gate 1, at 0 dB SNR no less) is physically impossible; realistic latency is >10 s. One sub-claim is model-dependent: 'consecutive evaluations see identical LOW data' assumes a fixed streaming STFT grid — a per-window recomputed STFT shifts frame boundaries each eval, so the exact 12.8 s figure is a heuristic, not a theorem. But the headline claim (every gate row violated under spec defaults; §22.1 and the M4 test cannot pass as written) survives, and it directly matters: M4/§22 assert these numbers in tests.
